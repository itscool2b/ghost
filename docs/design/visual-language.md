## Main

Main is the look Ghost opens in (owner, 25 November 2026: "this is gonna be called main theme ... a styling pass for the app in general, not just an appearance"). It is two things. First, a palette: Ghost's own neutral one, black, white and grey, in both appearances, and the default theme. Second, and for every theme: shape, type, spacing, icons and the message box. Its models are the Grok Bot desktop app, whose structure and pills the desktop already wore, and the Synara ADE, for density and precision ("their app looks really good all the way from the icons to the formatting to the message box"); [reference-audit.md](reference-audit.md) records both. Nothing of either ships.

- **One ink, three layers, no colour that means nothing.** The window under the sidebar, the pane, and what is raised, a step apart; fills and edges are the ink at set strengths. Accent, focus and selection are ink in Main. Colour is for meaning only: a diff's green and red, a status dot, an agent's own mark.
- **Small and light.** The interface is 13px, the chat 14px, details 12px. Headings sit one step under the Grok Bot app's (16, 20 and 24px) and rank is carried by weight and tone: medium for names and titles, regular for everything else, grey for what explains.
- **Thin icons.** Icons are Lucide's at a stroke of 1.5 in their own 24-unit grid, which is one pixel at 16px, and 1.75 at 14px and under (`--g-icon-stroke`, `--g-icon-stroke-small`). One rule in `graphite.css` applies it to every icon that sets no stroke of its own, so the Send arrow and a filled Stop keep theirs. Icons rest in tertiary text and take ink under the hand.
- **Three control heights.** 24px for inline actions, 28px for toolbars and the message box, 32px for forms and dialogs (`--g-control-sm`, `--g-control`, `--g-control-lg`). A row in a list or a menu is 30px (`--g-row`), and rows sit 2px apart.
- **Ghost first.** A control is bare at rest, takes the quietest fill under the hand (6%) and a deeper one while open or pressed (10%). One filled control per view says what Enter does.
- **Pills stay.** Buttons, chips, segmented controls and switches are pills, as in the Grok Bot app. A segmented control is a pill outlined by one hairline with the choice a flat step of ink inside it: no filled track and no raised thumb, in the shared primitive, in Settings and in the side panel's tabs, which have no track at all.
- **Menus and dialogs.** A menu is the raised layer at 14px with 30px rows at 10px, 4px in, so the corners stay concentric; a row's description sits beside its name in grey, and a part's name is one quiet line, not a heading. A dialog is 20px with its title at heading size.
- **The sidebar.** Rows are 30px. A chat shows its agent's mark in the gutter, on the project dot's axis, then its title, and at the end either what it asks of you (a dot or the loader) or how long ago it last moved ("4m", "2h", "3d"); a pinned chat shows its pin before that. The agent tile is a quiet plate with a hairline, never a strong ring.
- **The message box** (owner: "that needs to be more clean, like Synara"): the raised layer at 20px, the words on top with room for two lines, one calm row of controls under them. Every control in the row is bare at rest and 28px; Send alone is filled, a 28px disc of ink. The plus opens what can be added and turns to a cross while open.
- **The sent message** is a soft step of the ink over the pane in both appearances (10% in dark, 7% in light) with the ink for its words, at 16px. The Grok Bot app's solid block in light striped a long thread.
- **The tray.** What waits beside the message, its background tasks and its queued messages, sits on a sheet tucked behind the message box's top edge, after Synara's (`--ui-tray-fill`): a step narrower than the box (12px in at each side) and quieter than it, with 14px top corners, one hairline, and the box's corners standing over its foot. Two sheets stack the same way, and a request that takes the box's place stands over them as the box does. Rows on a sheet are 28px; a task is a bare pill on it, with a fill under the hand and the open step while its panel shows.
- **The box's menus open above the box, on its edge.** What can be added, how freely and in which mode open from the box's left edge; the account and the model from its right. Anchored to the box and not to its own control, a menu never covers the words being written, and the five share two edges. A menu's first lines say what it sets, as one quiet 12px line and its sentence.
- **Dialogs.** A field's label is quiet: grey and regular, 6px over its field, so what the field holds is the brightest thing. A focused field is one crisp line in the focus colour drawn over its own edge, not a second ring around it: a field takes focus whenever it is typed in. A dialog that is a form opens on its first field (`data-autofocus`, set by `showDialog` in `Modal.tsx`), not on its close button. Beside a dialog's one filled answer every other action in the row is a ghost; a row with no such answer keeps the soft pill. A field's one action sits beside it on the field's line.
- **Cards of rows.** A list inside a form or a pane (a project's folders, kept worktrees, a phone's devices) is one flat card at 10px with a faint fill and hairlines between its rows: the name, what can be done to it at the right on the name's line as 24px ghosts, and where it is in grey under them. A path is set in mono; words about it are not.
- **The project dialog** (owner: "when you create a new project, when you get like the little menu, it needs to just look amazing"): the name and its colour share the first line, the colour as four 14px discs with the chosen one ringed in its own colour; the folders are a card of rows with how to add one under it (a new project's chosen folder shows as a field that cannot be typed in); what new chats start with sits side by side; and the dialog ends on one line, what was removed before at the left as a ghost and the two answers at the right.
- **The website is X Money's, in its dark version** (owner, 26 November 2026: "copy MANY MANY ASPECTS OF IT"): see "Website composition" below. The polar.sh skeleton of 25 November is gone; the site stays dark.
- **The Dashboard's calendar is ink.** AI line edits draws its days in the theme's own ink at five strengths (`--g-heat` is `--g-fg-1`), where it was a blue: the one saturated patch left in a neutral window.

What Main replaces further down: the layer values, the radii, the type scale, the default theme, the sidebar's rows, the message box, its menus and its tray, the sent message, dialogs and their fields, the project and chat options dialogs, the Git dialog's wells and footer, the command palette's rows, the Dashboard's calendar, the website and the remote page's bubble. Where a paragraph below still describes one of these as it was before 25 November 2026, this section is the authority.

## Direction

Halo began as glass windows lit by each agent's brand glow. The owner retired every glow on 2 November 2026: "aura" in the name does not mean glowing colour, and none may be added again, on the website or in the app. Depth now comes from tone, hairlines, type contrast and real product imagery. The website is a plain page with rounded cards a step off it, black in dark and white in light (owner, 10 November 2026; see Website composition). The brand colours in `packages/ui/src/brand.ts` mark agents only (their logos and chips), never light. The desktop has no glow at all (owner, 2 November 2026: "aura" in the name does not mean glowing colour; never add one). Its live state is the working line in the transcript; nothing moves on the composer (owner, 6 November 2026, retiring the sea lion and turtle that swam along its rim). In Aura, Ghost's own theme, accent, focus and selection stay neutral, per the owner's 1 November decision; each agent's theme carries its agent's colour there instead (owner, 3 November 2026), and Claude Code's is the default (owner, 4 November 2026; see Themes below).

The website is built on X Money's frame in its dark version (owner, 26 November 2026: "copy MANY MANY ASPECTS OF IT"; "Website composition" below). Before that it was a close copy of dot.com, the Grok Bot landing page (10 and 17 November 2026), and then polar.sh's skeleton (25 November); earlier passes that aimed for a look of their own (a 104px bold headline, a serif italic accent, a floating capsule header, rounded night bands) were each rejected. Animation stays, where it shows the product working. The desktop keeps the audited Devin organization and, since 17 November 2026, wears the Grok Bot app's own look: flat solid layers, one ink at set strengths and no blur (see Desktop composition). Adapt patterns to Ghost's own product and capabilities. Do not copy reference brands, artwork or content, and do not imply unsupported functionality. The Ghost logo is a flying wing with two eyes (owner, 18 November 2026, with the artwork: "have this be the new logo everywhere"); it replaced the sea lion and turtle. `packages/ui/src/graphite/logoPaths.ts` is its one source: the artwork (`docs/design/brand/logo-source.png`) redrawn as straight edges with rounded corners, a small variant with larger eyes for 40px and under, and the outline and eyes as separate paths. The app icons (`pnpm --filter @ghost/desktop icons`), the favicons and the social card are generated from it. It is far wider than tall, so `Logo`'s size is its width. Do not add independent decorative animals, swimming or water motifs; the working companion that swam along the composer's rim was retired on 6 November 2026, so there is no exception. On the desktop the logo itself is alive in four places and nowhere else: setup and the tour (owner, 22 November 2026: "a sexy onboarding", and "a sexy ass tour with animations of the b2 showing u around"), the start screen and New chat (owner, 26 November 2026: "it should just be our logo in a really sexy way"), all described under Desktop composition. It is the shared `Wing` (`packages/ui/src/graphite/Wing.tsx`), the same one the website flies, so there is one bird. It never moves on the composer, in the transcript or while agents work, it carries no glow, and it holds still under reduced motion.

## No eyebrows

Never add eyebrows. That means no small label above a heading or section. It covers uppercase or mono micro-labels, numbered kickers such as "THE WORK / 01", "brand / section" lines, category labels and badges. Headings stand on their own. `.github/scripts/check-styles.mjs` enforces this for the website, the desktop and the shared primitives. It rejects uppercase text transforms, eyebrow, kicker and overline class names, and generated capitalized `content` labels.

## Shared foundation

`packages/ui/src/graphite/tokens.css` is the shared token source. `graphite/tokens.ts` and its tests mirror palettes where code needs them. Existing `--g-*` names are compatibility names, not permission to restore the former Graphite direction. Keep color, typography, spacing, radii, layers, motion, states, code and terminal values semantic and centrally defined. Do not introduce a parallel token system.

Layer tokens follow the Grok Bot app installed on the owner's machine, measured from its bundle on 17 November 2026 (owner: "go to Grokbot and essentially just copy it and adapt it to each of the styles that we offer"; see [reference-audit.md](reference-audit.md)). The `glass` in some names is historical: nothing is translucent and nothing blurs.

- **Layers.** Solid colours, one step apart: `--g-window` under the sidebar (`#0e0e0e` dark, `#f5f5f5` light), `--g-glass-layer` for panes (`#131313`, `#ffffff`) and `--g-glass-pop` for what is raised: the message box, menus, popovers, toasts and dialogs (`#1b1b1b`, `#ffffff`). Main's dark layers are a little inkier than the Grok Bot app's `#161616` and `#1f1f1f`, which they were until 25 November 2026. Each theme cuts its own four layers from these seeds (`themes.ts`).
- **One ink at set strengths.** Fills are the theme's ink over transparent: `--g-glass-card` (4% in dark) for cards; `--g-glass-control` (6%) for a control at rest and a row under the hand; `-hover` (10%) and `-active` (16%) a step and two further (8% and 12% in light); `--g-fill-primary-disabled` for a primary that cannot act yet (13% dark, 15% light). Edges are inset hairlines of the same ink: `--g-glass-rim` (8%) and `--g-glass-rim-strong` (14%). Text is `--g-fg-1` to `--g-fg-4`, solid colours solved to 4.5:1 on every layer.
- **Depth.** Dark draws no shadows, as the reference: `--g-shadow-control` and `--g-shadow-inline` are empty there, and tone and edges carry depth. Light lifts controls with `--g-shadow-control`, the message box with `--g-shadow-inline` (two soft layers) and menus, popovers and dialogs with `--g-shadow-pop`. Panes have a hairline (`--g-shadow-layer`), never a soft shadow.
- **No blur.** No surface blurs what is behind it. `tokens.test.ts` rejects `backdrop-filter` in the shared primitives, and the layout check (`check-layout.mjs`) fails on any blur, on a soft shadow under a box larger than 40% of the window (WebKitGTK repaints it with every change inside it when it paints on the CPU) and on a filled layer over the whole window other than a dialog's scrim.
- **Contrast.** `tokens.test.ts` and `themes.test.ts` require 4.5:1 for primary, secondary and tertiary text on every layer of every theme, in both appearances.

Radii: 8px for tooltips; 10px for inputs, sidebar rows and menu rows; 14px for panes, menus and popovers; 16px for cards, toasts and the sent message; 20px for the message box and dialogs; and pill for every button, chip, segmented control, tab, switch and picker trigger. A menu is 14px with 30px rows at 10px, 4px in from its edge. Nested radii stay concentric.

Geist Variable is the interface and prose face on the desktop and the website. Geist Mono sets machine-generated text. The website sets everything in Geist, the nearest face to dot.com's own grotesque, and loads no accent face (owner, 10 November 2026; the Fraunces Italic accent of 3 November is retired and its package removed). Emphasis on the website is tone, never a second face: ink for what is said, grey for what explains it. Desktop type takes the reference's roles, with Main's headings a step smaller: captions 11/16px, metadata 12/16px, chrome 13/20px, prose 15/24px, headings 16/24px, titles 20/28px and display 24/32px, with no tracking on body text and a little negative tracking on headings, more as they grow. The chat's own text (answers, sent messages, question options and the composer) is `--g-text-chat`/`--g-leading-chat`, 14/22px, and answers run the whole column instead of stopping at 600px (owner, 4 November 2026: fit more text in, as Codex does). Weights are tokens only, graded by platform and appearance as the reference grades them (owner, 17 November 2026: "look how thin some of the characters are"): 400/500/600 on Windows, 420/510/600 on macOS, and on Linux, where text is drawn at twice its size and scaled by the compositor, 430/530/620 in light and 450/550/640 in dark. `data-os` is set before the first paint. Keycaps draw the key symbols Geist Mono lacks (⌘ ⇧ ↵ ⌥ ⌃ ← →) instead of borrowing a system font, and timers, counts and diff totals use tabular figures. Settings > General > Text size offers Compact, the default, and Comfortable, which sets the chat at the prose size as before (`data-text-size="comfortable"` on the root). Keep success, warning and danger distinct and verify text contrast on its real surface.

Main's dark page and first four surfaces are `#131313`, `#171717`, `#1b1b1b`, `#212121` and `#272727`; the light counterparts are `#ffffff`, `#ffffff`, `#f8f8f8`, `#f3f3f3` and `#ececec`. Tertiary text stays `#959595` in dark: a darker grey was tried and measured 4.49:1 on a lit card over the pane, where the dashboard sets small text. Code, terminal, diff, selection and status tokens keep explicit values for both modes. Do not assume a dark code or terminal surface in light mode.

Desktop appearance defaults to System, with profile-local persisted Light and Dark overrides in the versioned setup preferences. Follow OS changes while System is selected. Keep the document root, native window and menus, portals and terminal palette consistent with the resolved appearance. The website follows the system appearance, as dot.com does (owner, 10 November 2026, choosing "Follow system" over always dark and always light; this replaces the earlier permanently light rule): dark unless the system asks for light, by `prefers-color-scheme` alone, so it needs no script. It has no appearance control and stores no choice. Website forms and third-party verification follow it too. One thing stays light: the hosted verification page, which the desktop shows inside its own dialog (pinned with `data-theme="light"`). The script-free standalone notices page follows the system too, in the site's colours. Preserve non-JavaScript behavior and the website's hashed content security policy.

## Themes

The desktop has a theme for every supported agent (owner, 3 November 2026: "multiple sexy ass themes ... a theme for every supported harness"). Settings > Appearance > Theme offers Main, Ghost's own neutral palette, then Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, Pi, Antigravity, Cursor, OpenCode, oh-my-pi and Hermes. Main is the default (owner, 25 November 2026; Claude Code's was from 4 November): a profile that never chose a theme gets it, and its first frame already wears it. Main is saved as `aura`, the name this palette had before, so files written by earlier builds still read. A profile whose saved theme was still the earlier default moves to Main once, the first time a build with Main opens it: until Main every save wrote the default out, so a saved "claude" could not tell a choice from a default. The file records that it has met Main, so choosing Claude Code's theme afterwards stays, and a profile that had picked any other theme keeps it. The website's product images keep Aura's neutral palette, which suits its plain page, and are captured in light and in dark. A theme is a palette layer independent of the appearance: `data-palette` beside `data-theme` on the document root, with its own dark and light sets, so System, Light and Dark work in every theme. It changes colour only, never type, spacing, radii or motion: layers, lines, text, filled controls, accent, focus, selection, code, terminal and diff colours, and the tint over the native material behind the sidebar. It adds no glow, and menus and popovers stay solid. Each is cut from its agent's own look without copying any interface or artwork: Claude Code is warm graphite and ivory paper with terracotta for what you act on; Codex is cool slate with an ice-blue mark; Grok Build wears the Grok Bot app's own colours in dark (a graphite window, a lighter chat over it, off-white text and paper for what you act on; owner, 10 November 2026) and stays black on white in light; Pi is charcoal and amber; Antigravity is indigo night with a violet mark; Cursor is ink with a periwinkle mark; OpenCode is a terminal at night, deep green-black and phosphor green; oh-my-pi is plum with a magenta mark; Hermes is warm black and gold. The gallery shows each theme as a small window painted in its own colours for the appearance in effect, with the agent's mark beside its name; a choice applies at once, arrow keys run through the themes without losing focus, and a failed save returns to the saved theme beside the error. The choice is saved in the profile beside the appearance; a missing or unknown theme reads as the default, Claude Code's.

`packages/ui/src/graphite/themes.ts` is the source of truth. `themes.css` is generated from it (`node packages/ui/scripts/write-themes.mts`), and `themes.test.ts` fails on drift or on any contrast the base palette keeps: text on the page and on every layer, outlines and focus rings, labels on filled controls, and code on its surface. A new agent gets a theme in the same change that adds it, or Aura stands in until it does.

## Desktop composition

The window shows its native material only behind the sidebar, under a tint:

- **Windows.** Acrylic on Windows 11 build 22523 and later, Mica on builds 22000 to 22522, and opaque on Windows 10. A window with a material hands its caption colors back to the system, so the caption and the page read as one surface.
- **macOS.** Vibrancy, or Liquid Glass where the system offers it.
- **Linux and browser fixtures.** No material: the sidebar sits on the opaque `--g-window`.

The layers (owner, 17 November 2026: the Grok Bot app's look, in every theme; "no glow bullshit, clean as fuck"):

- The sidebar sits on the window's colour.
- The chat (with its docked terminal), the settings pages and the Changes panel are solid panes, inset 8px with a 14px radius and one faint edge. When WebKitGTK paints on the CPU the edge is an outline (`data-paint="cpu"`).
- The chat header draws no line under it: the transcript fades out beneath it into the pane over 16px, held in the transcript's own scroller so a terminal's first row is never covered.
- The message box is the raised layer at 20px with one faint edge, lifted off the pane by the soft inline shadow in light and by its edge alone in dark. Focus steps the edge up and draws no coloured ring (owner, 9 November 2026); the caret says where typing goes. A request waiting on you takes its place with the same depth and a firmer edge.
- Menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts and dialogs are the raised layer, solid (`--ui-menu-fill`; owner, 2 November 2026), so what is being chosen reads clearly over any content.
- Grouped settings rows and transcript cards sit on flat cards (see the Grok Bot app's details below).
- The details of the Grok Bot app are the desktop's own (owner, 10 November 2026: "copy its super styling like be inspired and add it all over to the app. every small detail matters"; measured from the live app on its page, see [reference-audit.md](reference-audit.md)). They sit in the shared layer, so every theme and both appearances wear them:
  - **The sent message is a bubble**: a soft step of the theme's ink over the pane with the words in ink, 10% in dark and 7% in light (`--ui-paper`, `--ui-paper-ink`; until Main, 16% in dark and solid ink in light), one even 16px radius, 8px by 12px inside, no rim. What sits inside it reads from the same ink.
  - **Text sits at its natural spacing**: body text has no tracking, as in the reference; headings tighten a little. Code keeps its grid.
  - **Search is a field**: the sidebar's first row wears a faint fill and a hairline rim, 30px tall with a 10px radius, its word in the placeholder's tone. It is still the button that opens search, and in the rail it is an icon among icons.
  - **Add is a plus**: 28px and bare at rest since Main (a disc of ink at 6% before), a fill under the hand. Send is a 28px solid disc in the theme's primary (32px before Main); with nothing to send it is a faint disc of ink (`--g-fill-primary-disabled`).
  - **A state that asks for you is a badge**: in the header, Waiting on you, a failure and a sign-in that is needed are a 20px pill in a faint tint of their own colour, with the agent's mark where that app sets its dot. Working stays plain words.
  - **Cards are flat** (owner, 10 November 2026, leaving the choice to Claude): one faint fill of the theme's ink (`--ui-card-flat`), a 16px radius, no rim and no shadow. Grouped settings rows, the transcript's changed-files and status cards, the dashboard's cards, the Plugins cards, the agents list and the diagnostics panel. Hairlines still divide the rows inside one. Code blocks and diffs are flat wells with one faint edge. The message box, New chat's card and a question or permission card are the raised layer, since they are where you type or answer.
  - Answers stay unframed and run the column (owner, 4 November 2026); the Grok Bot app frames its agents' replies, and that one detail is not taken. Its avatars and two-line previews in the chat list are not taken either: taller rows show fewer chats.
- The open chat and the open settings page are a flat step of ink (10% in dark, 8% in light) with no rim or shadow; any row takes 6% under the hand and the deeper step while pressed. An open picker's pill holds the pressed step while its panel shows. No colored side bars mark selection. Where a fill would take colored or secondary text under 4.5:1, the selection is a rim without a fill, as on the selected changed file. The Git review panels are rimmed outlines for the same reason.

Design for a 1440 x 900 native window, minimum 960 x 640, with 52px clearance for native title controls. The sidebar is 256px expanded and 56px as a rail. At 1200px and wider, the Changes panel can resize from 320 to 560px; below that it overlays at up to 420px. The terminal remains persistent and resizable. The panel's Session tab ends with what the running session loaded, as its agent reports it (missing features B, 5 November 2026): MCP servers, each with a state dot beside its word (green connected, amber starting or waiting for a sign-in, red failed), then skills, plugins and subagents, each folded to its count; MCP servers open when one failed or waits for a sign-in. Changes reviews as well as reverts (missing features C, 5 November 2026): each diff line has a comment button in a narrow first column, shown on the row's hover or focus (always where there is no hover), with one Tab stop and the arrow keys moving between lines. A comment sits under its line in a glass-control card that keeps to the visible width however far long code lines scroll; its editor takes Enter to add and Esc to cancel, and focus returns to the line. A review bar above the revert footer reads the comment count with Discard and Send, or "Comment on any line" with Ask the agent to review; after sending it says "Comment sent.", "Review requested." or, while a turn runs, "Queued for after this turn.", and when the composer could not send it says why and holds the comments. Changes shows each change with three unchanged lines around it; a longer unchanged stretch folds into a band in the hunk colour (`--g-diff-hunk`) reading "Show 9 unchanged lines", which opens it in place, and a fold holding a commented line stays open. Once no agent works in the folder, each change's band carries a ghost Revert at the right of the visible width (the fold's band before it, or a band of its own), and its confirmation names the file, the line and the +/− lines that change back. The Git workspace dialog commits and pushes: Commit changes lists the changed files as checkboxes with Git's status code and the path (a renamed file as old → new), then the message and Review commit; Push this branch has Review push. Their reviews use the dialog's rows (branch, parent or remote, author or remote branch) with the message, stat or commits in its preformatted blocks, and say that hooks do not run and that a push never forces. Find in chat (Ctrl+F) is a solid bar at the transcript's top right, like the menus: the field, "3 of 12" in fg-3, previous and next as ghost icon buttons, and close. Every match is tinted with the selection colour and the shown one with the theme's action colour (`--g-volt`, text in `--g-bg`). A row the virtual list had not rendered is brought into view, and the transcript keeps following new output when the match was already in view. Account for long titles, narrow windows, platform title controls, keyboard and focus order, scrolling and no horizontal overflow.

## Motion and accessibility

The website's motion began as dot.com's alone: the opening arrives once and the product pictures play. On 18 November 2026 the owner asked for more ("animate shit, make the whole thing look awesome"), so the wing is alive and the page moves as it scrolls, still with no glow and nothing painted in light:

- **The wing.** It flies into the headline, hovers, blinks and looks toward the pointer (`apps/web/app/home/Wing.tsx`); a faint one crosses behind the statement, nose first, as the page moves past it; the closing one draws its own outline.
- **Arrivals.** The statement's agents land one after another with a small overshoot when their row scrolls in, as dot.com's bots do. Cards, download rows, FAQ rows and the agents' view rise 36px and 3% into place, and the workspace frame 56px and 8%, tied to the scroll itself by a view timeline. Only position and size move, never opacity, so nothing can be left hidden or fall under 4.5:1 on the way.
- **Where it does not run.** A browser without view timelines shows the page at rest. Reduced motion stops every one of these, and the wing sits still in its place.

It is built from named primitives:

- **Primitives.** The opening (the headline's words flip up 70ms apart from 45% down and -40 degrees under a 1200px perspective, the lines and pills settle down from 10px above out of a 12px blur, the window follows from 20px above, about a second each), typing and streaming, handoff pass and the terminal's print (once its card arrives, marked by the enhancement layer's `data-reveal="trigger"`). The bar's Download fading out while the hero's own is in view (under 1024px) and colour changes under the hand (150ms) are the rest.
- **Easing.** Expo `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for entrances, glide `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` for moving indicators, and dot.com's `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` for colour.
- **Pausing.** Ambient loops pause offscreen and in hidden tabs (the enhancement layer marks offscreen zones with `data-off` and the page with `data-hidden`).
- **Fallbacks.** Reduced motion shows static, complete compositions, and without JavaScript the page shows the final composition in the system's appearance. There is no canvas.

Desktop motion stays event-driven, with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`:

- Hover 120ms, press 80ms, popover 160ms in and 120ms out with at most 2px travel, panel 180ms. These sit inside the reference's 50 to 300ms range on its standard easing.
- Glides use 240ms. Pressed buttons scale to 0.97.
- Dictation: each microphone report slides the waveform one bar in `--g-dur-wave` (80ms), a new word clears from a 4px blur in 240ms, Finish's sweep takes `--g-dur-ink` (560ms) and Cancel's dissolve `--g-dur-dissolve` (320ms).
- The wing, in setup, the tour, the start screen and New chat only: one flight takes `--g-dur-flight` (640ms) on the glide curve, its first arrival `--g-dur-arrive` (1100ms) on expo, a row it brings lands in `--g-dur-land` (360ms), and at rest it hovers over `--g-dur-hover` and blinks twice every `--g-dur-blink`.

Reduced motion and the in-app motion setting remove movement and duration. State changes must stay understandable without animation. Preserve visible keyboard focus, native dialog behavior, accessible names, error announcements, readable contrast, and robust zoom and reflow.

## Governance and verification

For future visual work, read this document and [reference-audit.md](reference-audit.md), then inspect the shared tokens and production components. Until the owner locks Halo, visual artifacts are proposals. Record the owner's approval and artifact locations before treating them as golden. For a new feature, extend the semantic tokens and interaction patterns, show desktop and website states in light and dark, and preserve accessibility and behavior. Update this document when an owner decision changes the design; do not leave competing directions in plans or READMEs.

Run `pnpm check` and `pnpm build`, `pnpm --filter @ghost/web test:browser`, and the desktop `check:chat`, `check:setup`, `check:settings` and `check:customization` suites as appropriate to changed surfaces. `check:settings` includes `check-themes.mjs`: the gallery, keyboard, persistence, a failed save and axe for every theme in both appearances. Run `node apps/desktop/scripts/check-layout.mjs` in both engines, both appearances and both window sizes; it includes the paint checks (no blur, no soft shadow under a box over 40% of the window, no filled layer over the whole window but a dialog's scrim). Add `--glass worst` to repeat it over the worst material behind the sidebar on Windows and macOS.

Development previews take `&glass=acrylic` to emulate the window material over a colourful desktop. `&backdrop=worst` paints the worst plausible material instead. Verify both desktop themes, system switching, saved desktop preferences, portal and terminal updates, the website under a dark and a light system preference, keyboard use, reduced motion, responsive sizes and contrast. Browser fixtures and screenshots do not establish native macOS or Windows release acceptance; verify native materials, menus and title controls on supported platforms separately. Existing style checks enforce token usage; use objective checks for meaningful invariants rather than freezing arbitrary layouts.
